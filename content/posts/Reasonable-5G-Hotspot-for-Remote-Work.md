At the same physical location, these were my results:

| Setup | Speed |
|---|---:|
| TP-Link TL-MR100 4G router | 8 Mbps |
| Phone with an Airtel SIM, in 4G mode | 10 Mbps |
| Same phone and Airtel SIM, in 5G mode | 100 Mbps |

That makes a 5G phone a reasonable hotspot for remote work where I am.

For prolonged plugged-in use, my key requirement was **bypass charging**:
powering the phone directly from the charger to reduce battery cycling and
heat, helping battery and device longevity.

After looking at various phones, the **Realme Narzo 100X 5G** stood out.

Buying a **local-market 5G phone** also helps avoid the operator compatibility
headaches that imported 5G hardware can bring. Check support for your local
operators' 5G bands and network modes before buying.

It also connects to my **MikroTik hEX S** over USB. Once I enable **USB
tethering** on the phone, an `lte1` interface automatically appears in the
hEX S UI. A useful way to feed the mobile connection into the existing
network.

Once Starlink Mini is available locally, it could give us another connectivity
option for remote work.
